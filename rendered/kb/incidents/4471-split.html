What each one is

parser: the shared parser reads dates month first

Every tenant on file writes the day first. The shared parser reads every date month first, so day and month swap: a booking for 7 April goes out as 4 July, 2 December becomes 12 February. Reports come from every tenant and every way in: app, email and booking-v2 forms. This is the fix #4471 already queued. Ticket: parser.

hint: the booking-v3 form tells people to type the month first

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. At a day-first tenant people type what the hint says, month first, and the form reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. The symptom is the same swapped date, so the subject lines match the parser reports, but the code is different: the parser fix does not touch it. Only reports that came in through the form of a booking-v3 tenant (alder.physio, copper.studio, pale.harbour). The fix is the hint: print the tenant’s own order. Ticket: hint.

one-day: bookings land one day early

Nothing is swapped. Tuesday 12 May shows as Monday 11 May; Tuesday 6 October shows as Monday; a client in Lisbon got Tuesday and a client in Toronto got Monday for the same meeting; every booking in June is a day off; an invite arrived before the booking was made. A date is being moved across a time zone. The desk already read these as a one-day move (desk-notes.json), but the cause table on #4471 files them under parser by tenant and only footnotes them, so they never had a ticket. They stay counted there, the desk’s way, and are counted again here. The parser fix does not touch them. Ticket: one-day.

How the reports were counted

  1. One id, one report. The ticket export comes in overlapping pages: 427 rows, 15 of them repeats, 412 reports.
  2. Cause from the tenant, not from intake. causes.ts decides parser or hint from the tenant’s locale, its booking form and the way the report came in. Intake’s tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser: it tags every en-US browser hint, whatever the tenant runs.
  3. Counted the desk’s way. Reports the desk reads as a one-day move (desk-notes.json) stay filed under the cause their tenant gives them, as the #4471 cause table counts them, and are counted again as the one-day problem: they are in two rows.

All numbers on this page are computed at render time from the same data as the #4471 page (causes.ts through problems.ts, ProblemTable, ProblemCount), so they follow the export as the desk adds to it.

Why they ended up in one ticket

Decision 0001 merges reports whose subject lines match. All the problems produce the same subjects (“Wrong day on the invite”, “Month and day swapped?”), so the subject cannot tell them apart, and “when unsure, merge” put everything in #4471. Matching on cause (causes.ts, as the triage explorer already does) would have kept them apart. That change needs a new decision record from its deciders; 0001 stays as accepted.
